Scaling Digital CS for 5,000+ accounts

How a long tail of under-served customers became a segment with its own operating model, its own team and its own way of being looked after.

JustCall (SaaS Labs) · Team Lead, Customer Success (Scaled)

The operating model, before and after

The problem

JustCall's high-volume, product-led segment had more than 5,000 accounts and a big share of the company's revenue, but it had grown faster than any way of looking after it. When I dug into why these customers were leaving, the causes weren't one thing. Onboarding was thin, there were product gaps, support didn't have the capacity to reach everyone, and a fair number of customers were never a great fit to begin with.

Deciding who gets what

The first job was segmentation, so that we stopped treating 5,000 customers as one group. I set up predictive health scores and AI account insights so CSMs could see which accounts needed a person and which were doing fine with good lifecycle outreach, and I built a centralised engagement process with campaign governance and weekly reporting so every touch was planned rather than ad hoc.

Building the team around it

I introduced a CS Associate role for this segment, then hired and coached 10 associates over time and built a path for them into strategic roles, so the team had somewhere to grow rather than burning out on volume. With prioritisation and lifecycle outreach doing the routine work, each CSM could look after 50% more customers without the experience getting worse.

Fixing churn at the source

Some churn couldn't be solved after the sale, so I worked with Marketing to refine who we were bringing in. Alongside that, a customer intelligence project turned the pain points and use cases of 500+ accounts into a dataset that shaped our ideal customer profile, pricing and packaging, which meant the customers coming in were more likely to stay.

What came of it

Churn in a customer's first 90 days nearly halved, net revenue retention for the segment improved by 14% in relative terms, and each CSM covered half again as many customers as before. More than the numbers, the segment went from being everyone's leftover work to a team with a clear model, clear signals and a clear path for the people in it.
