Case study · AI in Customer Success

An AI-led outreach program for 7,000+ customers

Most AI outreach fails because the data underneath it is thin, so we fixed the data first and taught the system what good looked like before it sent anything on its own.

JustCall (SaaS Labs) · Program lead

At a glance

7,000+customers covered, across every segment
25%+open rates held after automation
~30%of the expansion pipeline target came from this program

The problem

JustCall had thousands of customers who would benefit from more of the product but who no account manager would ever have time to call. Generic campaigns weren't landing, and the obvious answer of plugging an AI tool into the CRM would only have produced generic emails faster, because we didn't actually know enough about most of these customers to say anything specific to them.

Fixing the data first

I wrote a research SOP and had 12 CSMs profile 500 accounts over three months, capturing each customer's persona, pain points, use cases, tech stack and sentiment. That dataset became the backbone for everything we later fed into AI, and it paid off well beyond outreach, because it helped us build the health score model, made account prep faster and cut CSM training from four weeks to three.

Running it by hand for a quarter

Before automating anything, I ran the personalised campaigns manually for over a quarter so we could see what actually landed and what didn't. That period gave us a campaign SOP and, more importantly, a set of guardrails that came straight from how customers reacted.

We never pitched AI as replacing people, because customers in Europe and Australia asked whether we were trying to get them fired. We consulted rather than sold, which also lifted open rates, and we never sent pricing to daily users who weren't admins or managers, since most companies keep pricing away from their employees.

Then, automation

Once the playbook was proven, I built the automation myself in HubSpot, Zapier and n8n, so the system could send personalised emails off lifecycle triggers. Humans stayed on strategic accounts, and CSMs had a window to opt any of their customers out before a campaign went, which kept the people closest to each account in control.

What came of it

Open rates held above 25% after we automated, and the program delivered about 30% of the expansion pipeline target, which our account managers then worked. It also meant the digital CS team could cover far more of the base without adding headcount, because the routine outreach was handled and people could spend their time on conversations that needed them.

The part I'd repeat anywhere is the order of things: research, then manual testing, then guardrails, and only then automation. Skipping straight to the last step is how teams end up with AI that's fast and confidently wrong.
